The Travel Packing Guide

Packing light is not about taking less. It is about taking things that all go together, so fifteen pieces make more outfits than thirty.

Start with a colour plan

Before choosing a single garment, choose three colours: two neutrals and one accent. Navy and white with rust. Black and grey with green. Beige and denim with red. If everything you pack is one of those three, every top will go with every bottom and you can dress without thinking.

The 5-4-3-2-1 method

For a week away, pack:

  • 5 tops: a mix of T-shirts, a shirt and one smarter option.
  • 4 bottoms: for example two trousers, one pair of shorts or a skirt, one extra.
  • 3 layers and extras: a jumper, a light jacket and one dress or swimwear.
  • 2 pairs of shoes: one to walk all day in, one smarter. Wear the bulkier pair to travel.
  • 1 bag: a single day bag that works for sightseeing and for dinner.

Underwear and socks are counted separately: one set per day, up to seven, then wash. For a different length of trip, the Trip Packing Planner adjusts the numbers.

Roll, fold or bundle

Roll soft items such as T-shirts, knitwear and underwear; rolling saves space and avoids hard creases. Fold structured items such as shirts and tailored trousers along their seams and lay them flat on top. Fill shoes with socks and place them heel to toe along the hinge side of the case, in a bag.

Heavy things go at the wheel end of the case, so they sit at the bottom when it stands upright.

Plan outfits, not items

Lay out each day’s outfit on the bed before it goes in the case. If a piece appears in only one outfit, leave it at home. Take a photograph of each combination; on a tired morning, the decision is already made.

On arrival

  1. Hang shirts and dresses straight away. Most creases drop out overnight.
  2. Hang anything stubborn in the bathroom while you shower.
  3. Keep a small bag for worn clothes so clean and dirty never mix.
  4. Wash underwear and a T-shirt in the basin on day three or four and you extend the trip by half.
