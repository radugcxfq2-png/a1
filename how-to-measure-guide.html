The Measure & Fit Guide

Three numbers and five minutes with a tape measure will save you more returns than any size chart ever could.

What you need

A soft tailor’s tape, a mirror and close-fitting clothes or underwear. Stand naturally, breathe out and do not pull the tape tight. It should lie flat against the body and slide a little when you move it.

The three key measurements

Chest or bust

Pass the tape under your arms and around the fullest part of the chest, keeping it level across the back. Arms relaxed at your sides.

Waist

Find your natural waist by bending to one side; it is the crease that forms, usually a little above the navel. Measure there, not where your trousers sit.

Hips

Stand with feet together and measure around the fullest part of your seat, usually about twenty centimetres below the natural waist.

Write the three numbers on your phone. You will use them every time you shop.

Two more that help

  • Inside leg: from the crotch seam to the ankle bone, easiest taken from a pair of trousers that already fit.
  • Sleeve: from the shoulder bone to the wrist bone with the arm slightly bent.

Reading a size chart

Every label cuts differently, so a medium in one shop is a large in another. Ignore the letter. Open the brand’s chart, find the row that contains each of your measurements and note the size. The Size Finder gives you a starting point when no chart is available.

If you fall…Choose
Between two sizes in a woven shirt or jacketThe larger; it can be taken in
Between two sizes in stretch knitwearThe smaller; it will give
In different sizes for top and bottomSeparates in each size
In different sizes for a dressThe largest measurement, then alter

Judging fit in the mirror

Numbers get you to the right size. The mirror tells you whether the cut suits you. Check in this order, because the first items are the hardest to change.

  1. Shoulders. The seam sits on the bone, with no dent below it and no pulling across the back.
  2. Chest and back. You can cross your arms without the fabric straining.
  3. Waist. A trouser waistband stays put without a belt and does not gape when you sit.
  4. Length. Sleeves reach the wrist bone; trousers touch the shoe without bunching.
  5. Movement. Sit, reach up and bend. If anything rides, twists or digs in, it will annoy you every time you wear it.

When to alter and when to return

Hems, sleeve lengths and waists are quick, inexpensive alterations and well worth doing on a garment you like. Shoulders, armholes and anything too small are not. If the shoulders are wrong, send it back.
